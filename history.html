Our story

90 Years of Curtain Calls

From a silent-movie house to the Alberni Valley’s home of the performing arts.

The Port Theatre, 1914

Before the Capitol, there was the Port Theatre on First Avenue, across from Alberni Hardware. Jack Warren opened it in 1914. It seated 200, showed films every Wednesday and Saturday night, and an Edison cylinder gramophone supplied the music for the silent pictures.

The Capitol opens, 1936

Talking pictures arrived in 1929. In the hard years of the mid-1930s, Jack Warren saw the need for a larger, modern theatre and bought the corner of Fourth Avenue and Argyle Street in 1934. He died in December 1935 and never saw it open. His wife Louise and son Harold took over.

The 450-seat Capitol Theatre opened with great fanfare on May 11, 1936, with Humphrey Bogart in The Petrified Forest. A 1937 newspaper called it a “community asset,” with air conditioning, a modern sound system and a large chesterfield in the foyer. It ran as a cinema until the late 1970s.

Harold E. Warren, Capitol Theatre owner for 50 years

Born in Port Alberni in 1914, Harold learned the movie business helping his father run the Port Theatre, and was just 21 when the Capitol opened. For the next 50 years it hosted thousands of movies and many local events. The Capitol was also the start of his business career, which went on to include the Paramount and Roxy theatres, the Beaver Valley Drive-In, the Village Theatre in Qualicum, CJAV radio and Alberni Cable Television. He sold his theatre business in 1986 and died in Port Alberni in 2005, at the age of 91.

Portal Players is born, 1978

In the fall of 1978, about twenty Port Alberni residents met in the North Island College gymnasium to talk about forming a drama group. Among them were Allan Markin, Gordon Scoffield, John Andrews, Margita Hartman, David Lord and Pam Aspinall. The college gym became their first stage, with risers built for effect and the staff room serving as the green room. Admission was free at first.

Through the 1980s the company staged production after production, including Funeral Tea, Street of Dreams, A Family Christmas (1984), a sold-out The Odd Couple (1985), Sing A Song of Sixpence (1986) and Cheating Cheaters (1988). Portal Players competed in the North Island Zone Theatre BC festivals, winning praise for “thoroughly professional backstage skills” and individual awards.

Buying the Capitol, 1989

Looking for a bigger home, the members set their sights on the old Capitol. In 1989, against the better judgment of some, they bought it, and never looked back. The roof was repaired, dressing and makeup rooms were built, the washrooms rebuilt, and lighting and sound installed. It took more than $150,000 in grants and donations, eight months and countless volunteer hours. Project manager Maurice Farn called it “the impossible dream.”

The new home opened on September 27, 1989, with Something’s Afoot, followed that Christmas by The Red Shoes, the first show to use the new curtains.

The 1990s and beyond

Highlights include Harvey, which drew a record 750 patrons despite a blizzard on opening night, Mark of Cain (1994), Beauty and the Beast, and Opening Night, winner of three awards at the 1993 North Island Zone Drama Festival.

A later bequest from Helen Ford funded a major renovation of the front of house, now the Helen Ford Lobby.
